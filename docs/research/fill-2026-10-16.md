# The fill of the dogfood wiki, 2026-10-16

The fill sends the repo to the wiki `wikifact` as change requests, so that the wiki holds what an agent asks about the repo before it has to open a file. The measure is the Overview of the console: the searches with no read fall, the facts the search missed stay low, and the additions per search fall as the gaps close.

## Method

Agents, no code. 1 agent per slice of files, 3 at most at once. Each agent sends 1 claim per request, with the file and the line as the justification. The pipeline is the judge; no audit of the requests.

## The 2 passes

About 1,400 change requests from the 3 doc layers, the README, the code standard, the decisions, the future file, and the whole of `src/`, `eval/` and `scripts/`. At 23:00 the queue held 1,432 requests with about 13 hours of wait.

## Seen during the passes

- 5 agents at once pushed the search p95 to 639 ms and gave 6 connection resets. All were resent.
- 2 agents cited wrong line numbers in part of their justifications.
- 1 agent stopped itself at a long queue and had to be told that the queue is no reason to stop.
- The machine slept overnight, so the queue did not drain. The worker drains it only while the machine is awake. The "submission" rows that arrive while no agent runs are the children of a split.

## Open

The results of the fill are in "Findings, 2026-10-27" below. The follow-up work is issue [#1](https://github.com/Arkased/WikiFact-dev/issues/1).

### The sample

The sample is random (seeded generator, seed 20261027) over the final claims: 30 of the 378 rejected requests and 20 of the 650 duplicate requests. I read each claim, its justification and, for a duplicate, the fact that it duplicates. I checked the claim against the repo at commit `1b9b09e2` or `d94e12f6` (the build during the fill). - Junk: the claim is wrong, vague, or not about the repo, or its justification is not a source line (a work report, for example). A true claim with a wrong line number is not junk.

Seen in the sample:

- The splitter is the weak step. All 365 compound rejects are children of a split. The splitter output was still compound.
- The fill agents did not keep the rule of 1 claim for each request. The pipeline split 900 of the 1,391 sent requests (65%) as compound. The claims were mostly true: the sample holds 2 junk claims in 50 (4%), and 24 of 50 are correct compound rejects.
- The judge rejected 5 of 30 true claims. The duplicate verdict dropped a detail or a correction in 3 of 20 cases.

### The growth

| Last fill request done (2026-10-20 20:21 PDT)   |      1,771 |      1,854 |
| Now (2026-10-27, equals listWikis)              |      2,037 |      2,207 |

The fill requests wrote 1,582 new facts, 185 new versions of existing facts and 74 deletions. 10 of the 185 new versions restored a deleted fact (+10 live). 73 of the 74 deletions removed a live fact (-73 live); the other deletion hit a fact that was already deleted (0). So the fill changed the live count by 1,582 + 10 - 73 = +1,519. The live count rose by 1,525 over the fill (246 to 1,771). The 6 extra live facts are 6 new facts that other requests wrote between 2026-10-16 20:00 PDT and 2026-10-20 20:21 PDT: 1 from a `lookupRepair` request (2026-10-17 14:52 PDT), 1 from a top-level submission of another agent (2026-10-19 17:05 PDT) and 4 from the children of a split of another submission (2026-10-20 20:21 PDT). "Facts ever" rose by 1,588 (266 to 1,854) for the same reason: 1,582 from the fill plus these 6. The queue did not drain on 2026-10-16 as planned. 1,017 of the 3,677 changes of the fill landed on 2026-10-20 between 19:00 and 20:00 PDT. The facts since the fill (1,771 to 2,037, +266) come from the later submissions of other agents and from tag repairs.

### The repeat pass (estimate only)

Baseline: commit `d94e12f6` (2026-10-16 22:54, the last build of the fill day). Since then `git log` shows 832 commits. The scope changed in 686 text files (532 without test files): 31,538 lines added and 13,479 lines deleted. Data files (`*.json`, result folders) are not counted. The fill agents cited a test file in 1 of 1,391 requests, so the estimate leaves the tests out.

The table lists 1,382 requests because 9 requests cite no file. The group "Decisions" is not reliable. At the fill, the scope held 1 file, `docs/decisions.md` (83 lines). Now `docs/decisions/` holds 169 records of 22 lines each. Records have headers and blank lines, so the ratio overstates them. Without the decisions, the estimate is 434 requests. With the decisions, it is 2,983 requests. Counted on added plus deleted lines, the estimates are 3,330 and 725.

The cost uses the table `model_calls` and `config/prices.json` (cache write 1.25 times and cache read 0.1 times the input price). The base is the cost of the non-fill submissions on the current settings. The method has 3 steps:

1. Take each top-level submission (no `parent_id`, `kind` `submission`, status `done`) that the store received after the last fill request finished (2026-10-20 20:21:34 PDT). 132 requests match. They are the requests that agents sent.
2. Add the children of these requests. The set holds 405 requests: 3.07 for each request sent.
3. Add the cost of the 1,506 model calls of the set: $42.05. This is $0.104 for each store request and $42.05 / 132 = $0.319 for each request sent.

A mean over the last 7 days is not a valid base. About 1,682 of the 2,342 submission requests that finished in the last 7 days (since 2026-10-20 12:44 PDT) are fill requests. They finished on 2026-10-20 on older settings. The non-fill submissions of the 7 days cost $44.91 for 660 requests ($0.068 for each store request). That value times 2.60 gives $0.177 for each request sent. It is also too low, for 2 reasons. First, it uses the split ratio of the fill (2.60), not the ratio of these submissions (3.07). Second, the 660 requests hold 255 requests that are not in the cost base ($2.86 together, $0.011 each). These 255 are 55 top-level submissions that agents sent on 2026-10-17 to 2026-10-20 (53 split, 2 accepted) and 200 of their children (146 accepted, 39 rejected, 15 duplicate). They are not fill requests: the worker started them on 2026-10-20 (the first at 19:06 PDT), and 229 of the 255 finished between 20:49 and 21:01 PDT, after the last fill request. The worker ran them on the older group path, which asks one group question for many requests (Q7B, the group claim check; Q5B, the group fact draft; or Q15, the reconciliation of the drafts against the wiki; all 3 were removed later) and writes the row of that call under one request. So 239 of the 255 requests have no `model_calls` row, and 17 group calls plus 21 single calls (38 rows) pay for all of them. Their cost for each request is low because one call served many requests, and it is not a cost on the current settings.

| Case                           | Requests sent | Cost at $0.319 for each request | Cost at $0.127 for each request |
| ------------------------------ | ------------: | ------------------------------: | ------------------------------: |
| Without the decisions          |           434 |                            $138 |                             $55 |
| With the decisions (upper)     |         2,983 |                            $950 |                            $379 |

The value $0.127 is the cost of the first fill itself: $177.05 for the 4,962 model calls of its 3,614 requests, divided by 1,391. It is the lower value. The fill ran on the models and settings of 2026-10-17 to 2026-10-20, and its requests split 2.60 times for each request sent. The later submissions split 3.07 times and cost more for each request sent.

The repeat pass of 434 requests costs $55 to $138. The repeat pass with the decisions costs $379 to $950.
